# Acceptable AI Use Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines acceptable and prohibited use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) by employees, contractors, consultants and authorised third parties of Austera Transport Group (ATG).

The objective is to enable productive use of AI while protecting ATG information, customers, employees, systems and operations.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to the use of AI for ATG-related activities, including:

- Generative AI assistants
- Enterprise copilots
- Large Language Models (LLMs)
- AI coding assistants
- AI-enabled productivity tools
- AI image and content generation
- AI research and analysis tools
- AI agents
- AI-enabled SaaS applications
- Internally developed AI solutions

This policy applies whether AI is accessed through ATG-managed systems or external services.

---

## 3. General Requirements

Users must:

- Use AI only for legitimate business purposes.
- Use approved AI services where organisational information is involved.
- Protect confidential and sensitive information.
- Validate important AI-generated content.
- Maintain accountability for work produced with AI assistance.
- Follow applicable security, privacy, data and records-management requirements.
- Report suspected AI-related security or privacy incidents.

AI must support human judgement rather than remove accountability.

---

## 4. Approved AI Services

Users must use ATG-approved AI services for organisational activities involving non-public information.

The availability of an AI service on the internet does not mean that it is approved for ATG information.

Users must not bypass technical restrictions designed to prevent access to unapproved AI services.

---

## 5. Information That Must Not Be Entered into Unapproved AI Services

Users must not enter sensitive ATG information into unapproved AI services.

Examples include:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Access tokens
- Certificates
- Personal information
- Confidential business information
- Commercially sensitive information
- Internal source code
- Security configurations
- Vulnerability information
- Internal architecture documentation
- Critical infrastructure information
- Privileged legal information

Where uncertain, users must seek guidance before submitting information to an AI service.

---

## 6. Personal Information

Personal information must only be processed using AI where:

- There is an approved business purpose.
- The AI service is approved for the relevant information.
- Appropriate privacy requirements have been addressed.
- Access is appropriately restricted.

Users must avoid unnecessarily providing personal information to AI systems.

---

## 7. Validation of AI Output

AI-generated content must not automatically be assumed to be correct.

AI may generate:

- Incorrect information
- Fabricated facts
- Non-existent references
- Outdated information
- Biased output
- Insecure code
- Misleading recommendations

Users remain responsible for validating AI-generated work according to its intended use.

---

## 8. High-Impact Decisions

AI-generated recommendations must not be used as the sole basis for significant decisions affecting:

- Customers
- Employees
- Safety
- Critical operations
- Financial outcomes
- Legal rights
- Regulatory obligations

Appropriate human review and approved governance processes must be maintained.

---

## 9. AI-Generated Documents

Users may use approved AI services to assist with:

- Drafting
- Summarisation
- Research
- Brainstorming
- Document structuring
- Meeting preparation
- Analysis

Users remain responsible for reviewing the final output before it is distributed or relied upon.

---

## 10. AI-Generated Code

Developers may use approved AI coding assistants where authorised.

AI-generated code must follow normal software-engineering processes including, where applicable:

- Peer review
- Testing
- Security scanning
- Dependency validation
- Secrets detection
- Change management

Users must not introduce AI-generated code directly into production solely because it was generated by an AI tool.

---

## 11. Intellectual Property

Users must consider intellectual-property and licensing obligations when using AI.

Users must not knowingly provide protected third-party material to AI services where doing so would violate applicable rights or contractual obligations.

AI-generated material intended for significant external or commercial use must be appropriately reviewed.

---

## 12. External Communications

AI-generated content used for external communication must be reviewed by an appropriate person before publication where required.

Examples include:

- Customer communications
- Public reports
- Website content
- Regulatory submissions
- Media communications
- Formal business correspondence

The person approving the communication remains accountable for its content.

---

## 13. AI Agents and Automated Actions

Users must not connect AI agents to enterprise systems without appropriate technical and governance approval.

AI agents must not be independently granted capabilities such as:

- Administrative system access
- Infrastructure modification
- Production deployment
- Bulk data deletion
- Financial transactions
- External communications
- Privileged data access

unless specifically approved and appropriately controlled.

---

## 14. Security Controls

Users must not attempt to:

- Disable AI security controls
- Circumvent content filters
- Bypass access restrictions
- Extract system prompts without authorisation
- Override security guardrails
- Manipulate AI systems to expose restricted information

Security testing may only be performed where appropriately authorised.

---

## 15. Authentication and Credentials

Users must never provide passwords, authentication tokens, API keys or other credentials to AI systems unless the solution is explicitly designed and approved to handle them securely.

AI must not be used as a credential-storage mechanism.

---

## 16. AI Research

AI may assist with research, but generated references and factual claims must be independently validated where accuracy is material.

Users must not assume that:

- Citations exist
- Quoted material is accurate
- Statistics are correct
- Legal requirements are current
- Technical recommendations are secure

Authoritative sources should be consulted where appropriate.

---

## 17. Meeting and Conversation AI

AI tools used to record, transcribe or summarise meetings must comply with applicable:

- Privacy requirements
- Confidentiality requirements
- Records-management requirements
- Consent requirements
- Information-classification requirements

Users must use approved services for sensitive meetings.

---

## 18. Third-Party AI Features

AI capabilities embedded within existing SaaS applications must not automatically be assumed to be approved.

Before enabling material AI functionality, consideration must be given to:

- Information accessed by the AI
- Provider data usage
- Model training
- Retention
- Permissions
- External integrations

Material functionality may require security, privacy or governance assessment.

---

## 19. Prohibited AI Uses

Unless specifically authorised, users must not use AI to:

- Circumvent security controls
- Gain unauthorised access
- Expose confidential information
- Generate or obtain credentials
- Impersonate individuals deceptively
- Make uncontrolled high-impact decisions
- Perform unauthorised surveillance
- Autonomously modify critical systems
- Process sensitive information through unapproved services
- Generate deliberately misleading organisational records
- Conduct unauthorised security testing

---

## 20. Responsible Use

Users must consider whether AI use could create inappropriate:

- Bias
- Discrimination
- Harm
- Misrepresentation
- Privacy intrusion
- Security exposure
- Operational risk

Concerns should be escalated through appropriate organisational channels.

---

## 21. Record Keeping

Where AI contributes materially to significant organisational decisions, appropriate records should be maintained according to ATG governance and records-management requirements.

Records may include:

- AI system used
- Material inputs
- Material output
- Human review
- Final decision
- Approval

Requirements should be proportional to risk.

---

## 22. Incident Reporting

Users must promptly report suspected AI-related incidents.

Examples include:

- Sensitive information accidentally submitted to an unapproved AI service
- AI exposing restricted information
- Suspicious AI behaviour
- Prompt-injection attacks
- Unauthorised agent activity
- Compromised AI accounts
- Unexpected access to enterprise information

Users should not conceal accidental AI misuse.

Early reporting enables appropriate containment and investigation.

---

## 23. User Accountability

AI does not replace individual professional responsibility.

Users remain accountable for:

- Information they provide to AI
- How they use AI-generated output
- Decisions made using AI assistance
- Compliance with organisational requirements

"AI generated it" is not sufficient justification for inaccurate, inappropriate or unauthorised business activity.

---

## 24. Manager Responsibilities

Managers should:

- Promote responsible AI use.
- Ensure staff understand approved AI services.
- Escalate higher-risk AI use cases.
- Support appropriate training.
- Identify repeated or material non-compliance.

---

## 25. Training and Awareness

Users accessing enterprise AI services should receive appropriate awareness covering:

- AI limitations
- Information protection
- Privacy
- Prompt security
- Hallucinations
- Responsible use
- Incident reporting

Enhanced training may be required for developers, administrators and users of higher-risk AI systems.

---

## 26. Non-Compliance

Non-compliant AI use may result in:

- Removal of AI access
- Suspension of AI services
- Required remediation
- Security investigation
- Governance escalation

Actions will be proportionate to the nature and impact of the non-compliance.
