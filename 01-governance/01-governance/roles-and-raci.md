AI Governance Roles and RACI

1. Purpose

This document defines the key roles, responsibilities and decision-accountability model supporting Artificial Intelligence governance across Austera Transport Group (ATG).

The objective is to ensure that AI governance activities have clear ownership and that accountability for AI risk is not unintentionally delegated to technology teams, vendors or AI systems.

⸻

2. RACI Model

The following definitions are used throughout this document.

Role	Definition
R – Responsible	Performs or coordinates the activity
A – Accountable	Ultimately accountable for the outcome or decision
C – Consulted	Provides specialist input before the activity or decision
I – Informed	Receives relevant information regarding the activity or decision

Each activity should normally have one clearly identifiable accountable role.

⸻

3. Governance Roles

The AI governance model includes the following key roles and functions.

Executive Risk Authority — ERA

Provides executive accountability for material enterprise AI risk.

AI Governance Committee — AIGC

Provides cross-functional oversight and approval of significant AI initiatives.

Business Owner — BO

Owns the business purpose, outcome and business risk associated with an AI system.

Enterprise Architecture — EA

Provides architecture governance and alignment with enterprise technology strategy and standards.

Cybersecurity — CYB

Defines and assures cybersecurity requirements.

Data Governance — DG

Provides governance over enterprise data used by AI systems.

Privacy — PRIV

Provides privacy assessment and oversight where personal or sensitive information is involved.

Enterprise Risk — RISK

Provides enterprise risk methodology, challenge and oversight.

Technology Owner — TO

Owns the technical lifecycle and operational technology supporting the AI capability.

AI Delivery Team — ADT

Designs, builds, configures and implements the AI solution.

Internal Audit — IA

Provides independent assurance over governance and control effectiveness.

5. Business Owner

The Business Owner remains accountable for the business use of AI.

Responsibilities include:

* Defining the business purpose
* Identifying expected outcomes
* Ensuring appropriate use
* Ensuring appropriate human oversight
* Owning business risks
* Supporting risk assessments
* Ensuring continued business relevance
* Initiating retirement when the capability is no longer required

The Business Owner cannot transfer business accountability to an AI provider or technical delivery team.

⸻

6. Executive Risk Authority

The Executive Risk Authority provides senior accountability for AI risks exceeding delegated governance authority.

Responsibilities include:

* Accepting material residual AI risk
* Approving Tier 4 AI initiatives
* Reviewing significant AI control exceptions
* Reviewing material AI incidents
* Escalating matters to executive leadership or the Board where required

⸻

7. AI Governance Committee

The AI Governance Committee provides cross-functional governance.

Responsibilities include:

* Maintaining governance oversight
* Approving Tier 3 AI initiatives
* Reviewing Tier 4 initiatives
* Reviewing material AI risks
* Reviewing significant exceptions
* Monitoring governance metrics
* Reviewing significant AI incidents
* Sponsoring governance improvements

The AIGC does not replace the accountability of the Business Owner.

⸻

8. Enterprise Architecture

Enterprise Architecture ensures AI solutions align with approved enterprise architecture.

Responsibilities include:

* Architecture assessment
* Reference architecture
* Architecture patterns
* Technology standards
* Integration standards
* Cloud architecture
* Architecture exceptions
* Technology lifecycle considerations

Enterprise Architecture works closely with Cybersecurity, Data Governance and technology teams.

⸻

9. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity defines and assures security requirements for AI.

Responsibilities include:

* AI security standards
* Threat modelling
* Security architecture
* Identity requirements
* API security
* Network security
* Model security
* Prompt security
* Security monitoring
* AI supply-chain security
* Incident response

Cybersecurity provides independent challenge but does not own the business risk associated with the AI use case.

⸻

10. Data Governance

Data Governance ensures AI systems use enterprise information appropriately.

Responsibilities include:

* Data ownership
* Data classification
* Data quality
* Data lineage
* Data access
* Training-data governance
* RAG knowledge-source governance
* Retention
* Data lifecycle

⸻

11. Privacy

Privacy provides oversight where AI processes personal or sensitive information.

Responsibilities include:

* Privacy assessment
* Privacy impact assessment
* Data minimisation
* Purpose limitation
* Third-party processing assessment
* Retention considerations
* Transparency requirements

⸻

12. Enterprise Risk

Enterprise Risk provides risk methodology and independent challenge.

Responsibilities include:

* AI risk methodology
* Risk classification
* Risk assessment support
* Risk aggregation
* Risk reporting
* Risk acceptance guidance
* Escalation

Enterprise Risk facilitates effective risk management but does not replace the accountable risk owner.

⸻

13. Technology Owner

The Technology Owner manages the technical lifecycle of the AI solution.

Responsibilities include:

* Platform ownership
* Technical implementation
* Availability
* Configuration
* Integration
* Monitoring
* Technical controls
* Change management
* Lifecycle management
* Technical retirement

⸻

14. AI Delivery Team

The AI Delivery Team implements AI capabilities within approved governance guardrails.

Responsibilities include:

* Solution design
* Development
* Configuration
* Testing
* Control implementation
* Technical documentation
* Governance evidence
* Remediation
* Production support

Delivery teams may identify and manage technical risk but cannot independently accept material enterprise risk.

⸻

15. Internal Audit

Internal Audit provides independent assurance.

Potential assurance activities include:

* AI governance framework effectiveness
* Control design
* Control operating effectiveness
* Risk-management processes
* Governance decision evidence
* Exception management
* AI inventory completeness
* Compliance with organisational policy

Internal Audit forms the third line and therefore does not design or operate first-line controls.

⸻

16. Segregation of Duties

AI governance must maintain appropriate segregation between:

Building the solution

and

Approving the solution.

For higher-risk AI systems:

* Developers should not approve their own security controls.
* Technology teams should not accept business risk.
* Vendors should not determine organisational risk acceptance.
* Security assurance should retain appropriate independence.
* Material risk acceptance must remain with an authorised organisational risk owner.
* Independent assurance should remain separate from control operation.

⸻

17. Decision Escalation

Where governance functions disagree on whether an AI system should proceed, the matter follows the escalation path:

AI Delivery / Technology Owner -> Specialist Governance Functions
Architecture / Security / Data / Privacy / Risk -> AI Governance Committee -> Executive Risk Authority -> Board / Executive Leadership

The escalation process ensures unresolved risk is explicitly accepted or treated rather than implicitly transferred to delivery teams.

⸻

18. Accountability Principle

The operating model follows a simple rule:

The team implementing AI is responsible for implementing controls.

Governance functions define, challenge and assure requirements.

The business owns the business outcome and associated risk.

Executives accept risks exceeding delegated authority.

Internal Audit independently assesses whether governance is working effectively.

This separation is fundamental to effective enterprise AI governance.
